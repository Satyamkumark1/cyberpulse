# PART A — PRODUCT OVERVIEW

## A1. Background

Financial cybercrime in India follows a compressed, well-understood kill chain. A victim is induced to transfer funds — through a UPI collect request, a fake investment portal, a phishing page, a fraudulent job offer, a tampered QR code or card credential theft. The funds land in a first-layer receiving account, are immediately layered across two to four further accounts, and are converted to cash at ATMs or cash-out agents, frequently in a locality that the syndicate uses repeatedly.

The defensive apparatus is well developed at the *reporting* end — the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal and the citizen financial fraud reporting system give citizens a channel and give police a case record. It is weak at the *interception* end. Between "complaint filed" and "money cashed out" there is a window during which the money is still traceable and still inside the banking system. That window is where value is created or lost, and it is currently unserved by any analytical tooling in the hands of a district cyber cell.

## A2. Problem Statement

Investigating officers cannot currently answer, at the moment of complaint registration, the only two questions that would let them intervene:

1. **Where** is this money most likely to be withdrawn as cash?
2. **When** is that most likely to happen?

They cannot answer them because the required signals — complaint attributes, transaction velocity, account linkage depth, historical cash-out geography, ATM density, and time-of-day patterns — live in different places, arrive too late, and have never been joined into a predictive model.

## A3. Opportunity

The signals needed to make these predictions are, in principle, already generated by the ecosystem. A platform that joins them and applies a well-specified ranking model can produce field-deployable intelligence in seconds rather than days. Because the model is tabular and tree-based, it can also *explain itself*, which is the precondition for an officer to trust it and for the output to survive scrutiny.

The opportunity is therefore not a new data source. It is a new **join, a new model, and a new interface** over signals that already exist.

## A4. Vision

Every cybercrime complaint filed in India automatically yields a ranked, explained, time-boxed cash-out forecast, delivered to the officer and bank who can act on it, within seconds of filing.

## A5. Goals

| ID | Goal | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| G-01 | Produce a ranked cash-out location forecast from a complaint | Top-3 hotspot hit rate ≥ 0.72 on synthetic holdout |
| G-02 | Produce a usable time window with the location | Predicted 2-hour bin correct within ±1 bin ≥ 0.75 |
| G-03 | Make every prediction explainable in officer-readable language | 100% of predictions return ≥ 5 normalised named factors |
| G-04 | Deliver intelligence as a dispatched, tracked action | Alert generation to persisted `SENT` state in ≤ 3 user actions |
| G-05 | Keep the system internally consistent | 0 screens displaying values that contradict their source API response |
| G-06 | Be demonstrable end-to-end in under 3 minutes | Timed rehearsal of `docs/demo-script.md` ≤ 180 s |
| G-07 | Be reproducible by a third party | Clean-clone setup to first live prediction in ≤ 30 minutes following `README.md` |

|---|---|---|

# PART B — USERS

## B1. Personas (summary)

Full detail, including a day-in-the-life narrative for each, is in `product/personas.md`.

### PER-01 — Investigating Officer, District Cyber Cell *(primary)*
Handles 15–40 open financial-fraud complaints simultaneously. Comfortable with case-management software, not with statistics. Judged on recovery and disposal rates. **Needs:** a defensible answer to "where do I send my team, and when". **Cannot tolerate:** an unexplained number.

### PER-02 — I4C Intelligence Analyst, CIS Division
Works national-level pattern detection across states. Data literate. **Needs:** aggregated geography, emerging clusters, and the ability to drill from a national map into a single complaint. **Cannot tolerate:** dashboards that cannot be filtered or exported.

### PER-03 — Bank Fraud Risk / Nodal Officer
Receives requests from many police units daily. **Needs:** alerts that are specific (accounts, ATM cluster, window), attributable, and arrive in a consistent structure. **Cannot tolerate:** vague, unranked, unsourced requests.

### PER-04 — Cyber Cell Supervisor (District SP)
Allocates people. **Needs:** throughput, severity mix, district concentration, and whether alerts led anywhere. **Cannot tolerate:** having to open individual cases to understand the picture.

### PER-05 — Platform Administrator
**Needs:** control of risk thresholds, model version visibility, data-mode visibility, system health, audit trail.

## B4. User Motivations

Officers are motivated by disposal and recovery statistics, by not wasting a scarce field team, and by producing case material that survives legal scrutiny. Analysts are motivated by finding the pattern first. Bank officers are motivated by reducing their own institution's exposure and by responding to police requests without re-work. Every design decision in this PRD is evaluated against those motivations rather than against abstract engagement metrics.

# PART D — PRODUCT SCOPE

## D1. MVP (hackathon deliverable)

The complete vertical slice: synthetic data → feature engineering → prediction → hotspot ranking → time window → explanation → map → graph → alert → investigation → reports → demo mode, with the role concept, settings, health and audit events. All 16 features FEAT-01 … FEAT-16 are in MVP; this is deliberate, because the value proposition is the *chain*, and removing any link breaks the demonstration.

Within MVP, build order is strictly phased so that the core prediction path exists before secondary surfaces are refined. See `implementation/development-strategy.md`.

## D2. V1 (post-hackathon hardening)

Real identity provider and enforced RBAC; state-level data partitioning; alert SLA and escalation; PDF case-handover pack export; model card publication; full audit log UI; PostGIS-backed spatial queries; per-state model calibration.

## D3. V2

Streaming ingestion (Kafka) for complaints and transactions; online feature store; continuous re-scoring as new transactions arrive; alert feedback loop feeding supervised retraining; outcome labels (was there actually a withdrawal in the predicted cell and window?).

## D4. Future Scope

Cross-case mule-network clustering and syndicate identification; federated learning across state cyber cells; drift monitoring and scheduled revalidation; integration adapters for NCRP/CFCFRMS/bank nodal channels subject to lawful authorisation; multilingual complaint parsing.

# PART E — METRICS

> All metrics below are **prototype and demonstration metrics** measured against synthetic data and rehearsal runs. None represents operational performance of a deployed system.

## E1. North Star Metric

**Actionable Forecast Rate** — the proportion of analysed complaints that yield a hotspot forecast with (a) risk level HIGH or MEDIUM, (b) a bounded time window ≤ 4 hours, and (c) at least five named contributing factors.

Target: **≥ 90%** of complaints in the synthetic corpus.

This metric is chosen because it measures the thing the product exists to produce — an intelligence unit an officer can act on — rather than usage of the software.

## E2. Adoption Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Prototype target |
|---|---|---|
| Complaints analysed | Distinct complaints for which `/api/predict` succeeded | ≥ 50 during rehearsal |
| Demo completions | `/demo` runs reaching the alert step | 100% of attempts |
| Role coverage | Each of LEA / BANK / ADMIN exercised in rehearsal | 3 / 3 |

## E3. Engagement Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Prototype target |
|---|---|---|
| Explanation open rate | Predictions whose factor panel is expanded | ≥ 80% in rehearsal |
| Graph open rate | Predictions where the money-trail graph is opened | ≥ 70% |
| Map drill-down rate | Hotspot markers clicked per session | ≥ 3 |

## E4. Conversion Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Prototype target |
|---|---|---|
| Prediction → alert conversion | Predictions that result in a dispatched alert | ≥ 40% of HIGH-risk predictions |
| Alert → investigation conversion | Alerts that produce an investigation record | 100% |

## E5. Reliability Metrics

1. All **Must** functional requirements in C1 pass their acceptance criteria in `ACCEPTANCE_CRITERIA.md`.
2. All NFR targets in C2 are measured and met, or a documented, accepted deviation exists in `project-management/decision-log.md`.

4. All Critical and High severity test cases in `MASTER_TEST_PLAN.md` pass.
5. Zero unresolved Critical or High defects.
6. `implementation/documentation-audit.md` reports full requirement→test traceability with no orphans.
7. `docs/demo-script.md` has been rehearsed end-to-end within 180 seconds, twice, on the target hardware.
