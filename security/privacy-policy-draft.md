# PRIVACY POLICY — DRAFT

> **NOT OPERATIVE.** This is a draft prepared so that a real deployment does not begin without one. CyberPulse AI as built is a prototype operating exclusively on synthetic data and has no users whose personal data it processes. Nothing in this draft is currently in force, and it has not been reviewed by counsel.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Version | 0.1 (draft) |
| Applies to | A hypothetical production deployment of CyberPulse AI |
| Current prototype status | No personal data processed — see `security/data-protection.md` |

---

## 1. Scope

This policy would describe how a deployed CyberPulse AI instance handles information about (a) the officers, analysts and bank nodal officers who use it, and (b) the individuals whose complaint and transaction data is processed within it.

The prototype processes neither. Every complaint, account, transaction, withdrawal and ATM in the current system was generated by software from a fixed seed.

---

## 2. Information That Would Be Processed

### 2.1 About users of the system

| Category | Purpose | Basis (to be determined by counsel) |
|---|---|---|
| Identity from the department's directory | Authentication, authorisation, accountability | Employment / official duty |
| Role, unit, jurisdiction | Access scoping | Official duty |
| Action logs — what was viewed, dispatched, changed | Audit and accountability | Legal obligation |
| Technical logs — request IDs, timing, errors | Operation and security | Legitimate interest |

### 2.2 About individuals in the case data

| Category | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Complaint attributes — type, amount, time, district | Prediction input | Location at district rather than point granularity |
| Account identifiers | Money-trail reconstruction | Pseudonymous references, minimised at the boundary |
| Transaction attributes — amount, time, channel | Prediction input | No counterparty personal details |
| Withdrawal and ATM data | Prediction input and label | ATM locations are not personal data |

**The system would not process, and its schema has no columns for:** names, addresses, telephone numbers, email addresses, government identifiers, real account numbers, biometrics, or device identifiers. This is enforced structurally rather than by policy (`security/data-protection.md` §2).

---

## 3. Purposes

Case data would be processed for one purpose: to forecast likely cash-withdrawal locations and time windows so that law enforcement and banks can intervene before funds leave the banking system.

It would **not** be used to profile individuals, to score people rather than locations, to train models sold or shared outside the deploying authority, for any commercial purpose, or for any purpose unrelated to the investigation of the complaint from which it arose.

---

## 4. Automated Decision-Making

CyberPulse AI produces **decision support, not decisions**. Every output is a ranked, probabilistic forecast about a location and a time window, accompanied by a confidence level and an explanation of the contributing factors. No enforcement action is taken automatically, and no output constitutes a finding about any individual.

A person affected by an action taken on the basis of a prediction would be entitled to know that a predictive system informed it, what factors contributed, and how to challenge it. The stored prediction, its factors, its ranked alternatives, the model version and the audit trail are retained precisely so that this is answerable.

---

## 5. Retention

| Data | Proposed retention |
|---|---|
| Case data | Per the deploying authority's statutory case-retention rules |
| Predictions and factors | Life of the associated case |
| Alerts and investigations | Per case-retention rules |
| Audit events | Minimum period required by applicable directions, then archived |
| Analytics events | 90 days, then aggregated and deleted |
| Technical logs | 30 days |

The prototype implements none of this. Absence of a retention policy is a declared gap in `security/compliance.md` §6.

---

## 6. Sharing

Alerts would be shared with named recipient classes — the relevant law-enforcement unit, bank nodal officers, and the coordination centre — as an inherent part of the product's purpose. Recipients see the alert and the case context reachable through it, and nothing more; that boundary is enforced server-side today (`security/authorization.md` §3).

No data would be shared with commercial third parties. The prototype uses no third-party analytics processor, no external error-reporting service and no external inference API.

---

## 7. Security

Described in `architecture/security-architecture.md`, with declared gaps in §10 of that document and in `security/compliance.md` §6. A deployment would require, at minimum: verified identity with multi-factor authentication, row-level access control by jurisdiction, audit retention, a breach-notification process, and an independent security assessment.

---

## 8. Rights

Rights available to a data principal under applicable law would be honoured through the deploying authority's existing process. Two tensions are recorded honestly rather than glossed:

1. Case records are append-only and subject to statutory retention, so erasure may not be available for them even where it would otherwise apply.
2. Disclosure of case data during a live investigation may be lawfully restricted.

Neither of these is a determination this project can make. Both require legal assessment.

---

## 9. Contact

A real deployment would designate a grievance officer and publish contact details. The prototype has none because it has no data principals.

---

## 10. Changes

This draft would be versioned with the application and any material change recorded in `CHANGELOG.md` under the `Security` heading.

---

## Note on Why This Draft Exists

A privacy policy written after a system is deployed is a description of whatever the system happens to do. Written before, it is a constraint on what the system is allowed to become. This draft was produced during Phase 1, alongside the schema, which is why the schema has no personal-data columns — the two documents were written against each other.
